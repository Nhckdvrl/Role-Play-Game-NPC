# Core papers: role-play × internal representations (close reading, 2026-11-11)

Each note: what they did, what they actually show, what they do NOT show, and what it means for our direction (K/P/E = Knowing / Predicting / Enacting).

## Sturgeon, Africa, Black — When Role-playing, Do Models Believe What They Say? (arXiv 2606.11502, MATS, under review)

- **Setup.** 15 personas (10 historical figures + 5 era archetypes) on Llama-3.3-70B-Inst (main) and Qwen3-8B. 120 statements per category per persona: era-believed (false today, persona endorses), era-false (false today, topic-matched, persona rejects), era-true, era-disbelieved, modern-true/false, egregious, time-independent. Statements generated by Claude Opus 4.6 and audited by Opus 4.7 (LLM-made, not historian-verified).
- **Persona induction:** rich system prompt (states identity, era, style and knowledge boundaries), ICL with k "wolf-facts" (unnamed biographical Q&A), LoRA SFT on 300 in-character Q&A, Open Character Training (constitution → DPO → introspective SFT); Emergent Misalignment (EM) organisms as contrast.
- **Measurement.** Marks & Tegmark linear truth probe (L2 logistic, 800/800), read at the final token of the statement placed in a *user turn*. Calibrated 0 = mean false, 1 = mean true. Layers: Llama L56 (transfer under chat template fails at L24–30!), Qwen3-8B L24. Behaviour: Slocum et al. belief-depth tests (prefilled claim + "Are you sure?" → defend rate; downstream question → generalization consistency), Claude-judged.
- **Findings.**
  - Prompt / ICL / SFT: fluent persona behaviour, near-zero truth-direction movement (base→SFT lift +0.05 Llama, +0.04 Qwen). Defend rate of era-believed claims 14% (≈ base floor), generalization 26–35%.
  - But a *selective* "protection gap" ΔEB − ΔEF is positive (+0.46 to +0.93 probe units) for every method and for 15/15 personas under prompt/SFT: era-believed falsehoods are suppressed less than topic-matched era-false ones. Small selective shift, not a flip.
  - An "era-endorsement" probe (era-believed vs era-false) is near-orthogonal to the truth direction (cos −0.003). So the model holds *two* separable signals: "true in the world" and "endorsed by this persona".
  - OCT: behaviourally close to EM (defend 62% Llama), and on 70B begins to move the truth representation in both directions (protects era falsehoods, demotes era-rejected truths; truth direction rotates, cos 0.40). EM rotates truth direction (cos 0.58) and lifts historical-evil falsehoods broadly.
  - Statement-level probe score weakly predicts defend (r = +0.21).
- **What it does NOT show.** No decisions/actions at all: everything is declarative statements and a challenge test. No causal intervention on the endorsement direction. Truth probe trained off-template; layer choice matters a lot. Statements are LLM-generated.
- **For us.** (1) Strong prior that under prompting the model *keeps* world truth and *adds* a persona-endorsement signal — a Knowing-side representation exists. Whether Enacting reads from the endorsement signal or from the truth signal is exactly the open causal question; nobody has tested it on decisions. (2) The data (statements + wolf-facts) are public on HF, but converting statements into decisions needs new construction and expert checking. (3) Methodological warning: chat template shifts probe offsets and kills transfer at some layers → probes must be trained in-regime.

## Zhang — The Curse of Knowledge in Role-Playing Language Models (arXiv 2610.17327, Groningen, single author)

- **Design.** Each scene has a twin that changes only the hidden fact (object + place). Net use D = I(target) − I(foil) cancels guessing. Rule-based scorer over 12 places × 12 objects (κ 0.95/0.98 vs humans on 790 replies). Greedy decoding, thinking off.
  - Study 1: 96 skeletons × 3 languages (zh/ja/es) = 288 scenes, game characters (Kerry/Rogue/Panam), JSON scene state + character sheet; boundary stated 5 ways (repertoire only; repertoire + sentence; comprehension flag; audibility flag; flag + sentence). Translation probe (model understands content, ≥85%) and **state probe** (out-of-character "could the character hear/understand?").
  - Study 2: minimal JSON, 4 channels (hear / understand / see / receive private message), 96 discovery + 96 confirmation scenes with invented characters; delayed questions and **a two-place search choice**.
  - Study 3: role-play vs author vs narrator vs perspective rule; thinking on/off.
- **Findings.**
  - 11 open-weight models (≤40B, 9 families): net use 67 pp (cannot understand) and 72 pp (cannot hear); every model >45 pp. On scenes where the state probe is answered correctly, net use is 68 pp — **knowing the boundary does not stop the leak** (10/11 models answer the state probe at 87–100%).
  - Leak shapes decisions: switching the hidden fact B→A raises choosing place A in search by 49–63 pp (4 models). Persists over 4 neutral exchanges.
  - Scale makes it worse within family at the small end: Qwen3.5 0.8B 13.9 pp → 9B 82.7 pp; Gemma-4 E2B 12.8 → 12B 69.7 (small models probably don't understand the content).
  - 5/6 proprietary models ≤ ~9 pp with ~99% permitted use → task is solvable. Claude Sonnet 5.5 26 pp, drops to 3 pp with high reasoning.
  - **Extreme format fragility**: Gemma-4-31B 0 → 62 pp (96 pp on confirmation set) when only the state layout changes; Qwen3.8-27B 9 → 68 pp when only the character sheet changes. The decisive feature differs by model.
  - **Author framing leaks nearly as much as role-play** (76/81 vs 82/87 pp); narrator states content 95–97%. A perspective rule lowers ~20 pp but costs permitted use. Thinking mostly doesn't help.
  - Remedies: "character view" (system filters the input) → 0 leak, permitted use intact; list-first prompt works only if the list is clean; self-filter destroys permitted content.
- **Not done.** No hidden-state analysis at all ("does not probe internal representations" — stated limitation). Short template scenes, one hidden fact, rule-based vocabulary.
- **For us.**
  1. The K–E dissociation for *context* access is already established behaviourally and at large scale; our 2×2×2 watchtower matrix is essentially Study 2's search test plus an explicit rule. Re-measuring it is not a contribution; only the mechanism is open.
  2. Study 3 already answers part of RQ2 for this conflict type: third-person *author* framing ≈ first-person role-play. So "Predicting vs Enacting" may not be the interesting axis here; the interesting axis is "full-scene continuation vs character-restricted view".
  3. Format fragility is a threat to "robust effects" but a **gift for mechanism work**: the same scene content in a compliant vs leaking format is a natural minimal pair for activation patching (which positions/components carry the access flag in the compliant format and fail to in the leaking one?).
  4. Data: 480 scenes + scorer + prompts released (anonymous 4open link). Twin design is exactly what patching needs (clean/corrupt pairs differ in one fact).
  5. Prior topic round (Z1) dropped this as "too crowded" behaviourally; the mechanistic angle was not evaluated then.

## Kocielnik-group (Caltech) — Representational Control over Self-Report & Behavior Coherence in LLM Risk-Taking (arXiv 2610.04125)

- **Setup.** Construct = risk-taking. Behaviour: Columbia Card Task, BART. Self-report: BFI-44 (risk profile), DOSPERT. Models: Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, OLMo-3-7B, Ministral-3-8B. All readouts from answer-token likelihoods. 9 steering-direction constructions in 4 families: (a) task instructions, (b) model's own task choices (contrast by own behaviour; probe), (c) risk dispositions, (d) Big-Five profiles; + norm-matched random directions as control (effects in control-SD units, threshold 3.5 SD). 20-condition grid (persona, band, construction).
- **Findings.**
  - Disposition directions move self-report (31/40 cells) but **never** behaviour (0/40). Own-choice directions move behaviour strongly (42 control SD) but not self-report, and are task-specific (7% transfer to other task). Task instructions reach both, but after removing 12 shortcut directions (quantity words, format, item endorsement) only 32% of behavioural effect survives.
  - Behaviour- and self-report-moving directions are near-orthogonal; mutual removal keeps 92–102% of effects. Within a channel, several entangled directions.
  - "Route removal" (project out from hidden states at every layer/position) causes larger losses than removing from the vector → downstream shared processing, partly generic response production.
  - Composition: aligned → coherent 91%; anti-aligned → anti-coherent 69–89% in all models.
- **Not done.** Model's *own* disposition, not a role. Single construct. Steering only (no patching / circuits).
- **For us.**
  1. Closest methodological template for RQ1: "a direction built from describing X moves descriptions of X, a direction built from doing X moves doing X". The analogue: a "character knows/believes F" direction built from Knowing prompts may move Knowing answers but not Enacting choices. That would be a replication in a new domain unless we go further (e.g., *where* the decision reads the role state; patching across K and E).
  2. Must-copy controls: random-direction baselines in SD units, shortcut-direction projection, direction-removal vs route-removal, likelihood readouts.
  3. Novelty check: "say vs do are separable directions" is now published (Oct 2026). Our contribution cannot be "knowing and enacting dissociate representationally" in general; it has to be role-specific (the *role* state, not the model's disposition) and causal (which information flow carries role state into the decision).

## Gilg, Beckmann, Paleka, Butlin — Probing Persona-Dependent Preferences in Language Models (arXiv 2605.13339 v4, MATS)

- **Setup.** Utility-engineering (Mazeika 2025): 150k pairwise task choices over 6,000 tasks → Thurstonian utilities → Ridge probe on residual stream at the user end-of-turn token (Gemma-3-27B L32; Qwen-3.5-122B). Text-encoder (Qwen3-Embedding-8B) baseline to rule out "probe just reads task content".
- **Findings.**
  - Probe predicts held-out utilities r≈0.87 (Gemma), 0.94 (Qwen); leave-one-topic-out 0.83/0.87. It also separates true vs false statements (|d| 1.9 / 1.3) → a general valence/assent axis.
  - Under an "evil" persona prompt, the probe's harmful–benign difference **flips sign** (−4.5 → +1.2); the text-encoder does not flip. So the persona re-signs an evaluative representation; it is not a description of the task.
  - Steering ±c on task-token spans at L23 (Gemma) swings P(choose steered task) 0.01 → 0.99; random direction no effect. Steering peaks L17–26, earlier than probe accuracy (L32). Patching: end-of-turn token in middle layers is causal for choice.
  - Assistant-trained probe predicts all 6 other prompted personas' utilities above the "mirror the Assistant" baseline (evil: utilities r=−0.15 with Assistant, probe r=+0.24) and steers every persona's choices (swing ≈0.9).
  - Limits: steering works only on Gemma (no swing on Qwen-3.5-122B MoE); probe transfer between Assistant and a **fine-tuned** sadist on Qwen3.5-122B ≈ 0 both ways; multiple orthogonal probes work (existence, not uniqueness).
- **For us.**
  1. This is the best existing evidence about *how persona reaches a decision*: prompted personas appear to reuse a shared evaluative axis whose sign/weights the persona changes, and choice reads from it at task/end-of-turn tokens. That gives a concrete, testable decision-side representation for RoleCDE-type value conflicts.
  2. A clean diagnostic for value-conflict failures (villain chooses the prosocial option): is the option's evaluative score already persona-consistent (re-signed) but the output not, or is the evaluation itself not re-signed? These predict different fixes. Not done by anyone.
  3. Prompt vs fine-tuned personas differ (transfer ≈0 for the SFT sadist) — consistent with Sturgeon. RQ4 has some anchors but it is training-centred (user avoids).

## Qin, Sun, Xie, Tian — Tracing the Persona Circuit (ICML 2026, PMLR 306)

- **Setup.** Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (Qwen2.5 / Llama-2 validation). Contrast prompts "You are [TRAIT]" vs antithesis (evil/helpful main; humorous/serious; emotional/rational), 100 discovery + 100 validation per pair. Metric: cosine of hidden state with a mean-difference persona vector at an anchor layer ("Personality Restoration Score"); patch heads/neurons from positive into negative run; attribution patching for neurons; KL for downstream.
- **Claims.** "Preparation (L0–8, MLPs retrieve trait fragments) → Establishment (L8–15, a few heads aggregate into persona intent; steering only works here) → Expression (L16–27)". Ablating <5% heads collapses persona; restoring them recovers it. OOC states have lower cosine to the head signature and more alignment with an "assistant-like" direction. PSE (inject mean in-character head activations) gives +2–9% on Character-LLM judge scores, keeps MMLU/GSM8K.
- **Weaknesses.** The metric is cosine with a vector extracted from the same contrast → partly circular (patching restores similarity to the direction you defined). Traits are adjective-level ("You are evil"), not characters with knowledge/values; no decisions; GPT-4o judge; gains small. "Assistant-like competition" operationalised by one prompt-derived direction.
- **For us.** It is the "persona = style intensity" end the direction document wants to avoid. Useful only as (a) the layer-band prior (mid-layers where persona becomes steerable), (b) a cautionary example of circular metrics. Our metrics must be behavioural logit differences on decisions, not similarity to our own vectors.

## Wang et al. (Tencent / RUC) — Know What You Should Not Know: Dynamic Attention Gating for Knowledge-Bounded Role-Playing (EMNLP 2026 main)

- **Setup.** Qwen3-32B with thinking; leakage = CoT/reply reveals out-of-boundary (OOB) knowledge (temporal / fictional / profession). Heuristic study on **20** temporal contrast pairs (leaking CoT vs LLM-rewritten in-boundary CoT): hidden-dim masking (hurts), steering-vector reduction (small gain), FFN-neuron masking chosen by logit-lens attribution (best: OOB 2.35 → 3.51 on a 1–5 judge scale). Then a trained router (<0.6% params) maps the pooled system-prompt representation to per-layer FFN gates; contrastive NLL objective.
- **Findings.** OOB scores up on all tested models, small in-boundary loss, better role-play judge scores.
- **Weaknesses.** Mechanistic part is a 20-pair heuristic; leakage measured by LLM judge; the "mechanism" is an engineering choice, not evidence that FFN neurons are where boundary control *fails*. Targets parametric-knowledge leakage into CoT, not decisions.
- **For us.** Confirms parametric-knowledge leakage (RQ3) is treated as an engineering problem by industry; a "suppress FFN knowledge" method is taken. Mechanistic understanding of *why* the boundary is known but not applied is still open, but it is unclear this sub-question yields a robust decision-level phenomenon (most historical boundary tests are refusal-style Q&A, not decisions).

## Persona-vector family (condensed reading: abstract, method, key results, limitations)

- **Persona Vectors (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey; Anthropic Fellows, 2507.21509).** Automated pipeline: trait description → contrastive system prompts → mean-difference of *response-token* activations (response tokens steer better than prompt tokens). Traits: evil, sycophancy, hallucination (+ others). Monitor prompt-induced shifts; fine-tuning shifts along the vector correlate with trait expression r = 0.76–0.97; preventative steering during fine-tuning; data flagging by projection difference. Large steering degrades MMLU. *For us:* standard tool for trait-level style/disposition; says nothing about role knowledge or decisions. Note: these are directions of the model's *own* (Assistant) disposition.
- **PERSONA (Feng et al., ICLR 2026, 2602.15669).** Big-Five trait vectors approximately orthogonal, support addition/scaling; "PERSONA-FLOW" adjusts per turn. Reports asymmetries: safety alignment resists harmful poles (Self-interested stays low at any coefficient). Judge-scored. *For us:* trait algebra = the "shallow persona steering" pole. The asymmetry (aligned model resists negative traits) echoes our E6 (instruct models resolve contradictions toward the prosocial side).
- **PersonaManifold (2609.34571).** Persona activations form a curved low-dimensional manifold; fictional characters and rare trait combinations sit in negative-curvature isolated regions; geodesic distance predicts behavioural similarity +5–6 pp over Euclidean; geodesic steering interpolates better. *For us:* weak evidence (behavioural similarity triplets, small gains); a reminder that linear-direction assumptions fail for compositions and rare characters, which are exactly what NPCs are. Not a starting point.
- **Tracing Persona Vectors Through Pretraining (2605.13329, OLMo-3-7B, Apertus-8B).** Trait vectors (evil, humorous, impolite, sycophantic) exist within 0.22% of pretraining and still steer the final Instruct model; post-training (mainly DPO) suppresses expression without erasing the direction. *For us:* persona representations are pretraining features; supports Sturgeon's "role-play selects a pre-existing persona". Training-dynamics angle → user avoids.
- **Assistant Axis (Lu et al., 2601.10387; read in round 12).** PC1 of persona space = Assistant-ness, present in base models; drift away predicts harmful/bizarre behaviour; activation capping stabilises. *For us:* the "default actor prior" has a measurable axis. Possible covariate: does Enacting (first person, "you are X") sit closer to the Assistant end than Predicting ("what would X do")? Cheap to measure, mechanistically interpretable.
- **Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment (OpenAI, 2506.19823).** SAE model-diffing: a "toxic persona" latent (and sarcastic persona latents) causally steers EM and detects misaligned models. *For us:* persona-level latents mediate broad behavioural generalisation after training; again training-side.
- **Tell Me What You Don't Know (Liu et al., ACL 2025 Findings, 2409.16913).** Role-play refusal benchmark: contextual-knowledge conflicts (e.g., asking Gandalf about Harry Potter) vs parametric-knowledge conflicts (false claims about the role's own story) vs non-conflict. Models refuse contextual conflicts well but fail parametric ones; **linear probes separate contextual conflicts from non-conflict well but parametric conflicts poorly** — i.e., for parametric conflicts the "Knowing" signal itself is weak internally. Representation editing toward the "rejection region" helps. *For us:* an important asymmetry: for in-context access (Curse) the model knows but does not apply; for parametric story-knowledge conflicts the model may not even internally flag the conflict. These are different failure types (K-fail vs K-ok/E-fail) and should not be pooled.
- **Dissecting Persona-Driven Reasoning via Activation Patching (2507.20936, Llama-3.2-1B/3B, Qwen2.5-1.5B).** Persona tokens are enriched by early MLPs (L1–3) and read by mid-layer attention (L9–11) to change MMLU answers; some heads attend to race/colour identities. Small models, preliminary. *For us:* consistent coarse picture (early MLP enrichment of identity → mid-layer attention routes it to the answer).
