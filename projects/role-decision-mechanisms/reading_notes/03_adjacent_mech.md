# Adjacent mechanistic work (ToM, binding, knowledge conflict, decodability vs causality)

## Prakash, Shapira, Sen Sharma, Riedl, Belinkov, Rott Shaham, Bau, Geiger — Language Models use Lookbacks to Track Beliefs (ICLR 2026, 2505.14685)
- CausalToM: two characters each change the state of an object; optional visibility sentence ("Bob can/cannot see Carla"). Llama-3-70B-Instruct (also Llama-3.1-405B, Qwen2.5-14B >80%).
- Mechanism via causal abstraction + interchange interventions: characters and objects get **ordering IDs (OIs)**; a **binding lookback** binds character–object–state OIs; an **answer lookback** retrieves the state payload at the final token via a pointer/address match in QK space; with a visibility sentence, a **visibility lookback** creates a visibility ID linking observer and observed and copies the observed character's information into the observer. Specific layer bands (e.g., answer pointer L34–52, payload only after L56).
- **For us:** this is the most precise known mechanism for "who perceived what". The Curse finding (state probe right, enactment leaks) can be posed mechanistically: when the model *speaks as* the non-perceiving character, does the final position query the character-conditioned belief pointer (visibility lookback) or the world-state payload directly? Interchange interventions on the access sentence vs on the fact tokens can separate "fact content flow" from "access control flow" — exactly the direction document's matrix, but with an established method behind it.

## Zhu, Zhang, Wang — Language Models Represent Beliefs of Self and Others (ICML 2024, 2402.18496)
- BigToM; linear probes on attention-head activations decode oracle belief (most heads) and protagonist belief (a subset of mid-layer heads, >80%); joint 4-way. Steering along the protagonist-true/oracle-false direction improves ToM on Forward Belief / Forward Action / Backward Belief; steering only toward "protagonist belief" fails ("overpowered by oracle belief signals").
- **For us:** the oracle (world) signal dominates most heads; the character-belief signal is in a minority of heads → a representational prior for why world facts leak. Also: Forward Action (predict action) is harder than belief and benefits from intervention.

## Feng & Steinhardt — How do LMs Bind Entities in Context? (ICLR 2024, 2310.17191)
- Binding-ID mechanism: entities and attributes carry additive binding-ID vectors in a shared subspace; queries retrieve the attribute with the matching ID; ubiquitous in large LLaMA/Pythia; transfers across tasks.
- **For us:** "character ↔ what it knows" in context is a binding problem; role state could be represented as attributes bound to the character entity. Mean interventions on binding IDs are a ready tool.

## Jin et al. — Cutting Off the Head Ends the Conflict (ACL 2024, 2402.18154)
- Context vs parametric memory conflict (World Capital): early FFNs enrich, late MHAs move info to the last token; **memory heads** vs **context heads** with opposite effects; path-patching-identified head pruning shifts reliance either way (+44 pp memory, +38.5 pp context) on 8 LMs.
- **For us:** a template for RQ3 (parametric knowledge vs role-allowed knowledge) and a reminder that "which source wins" can be localized to late heads in simple tasks. Role-allowed vs model-known is a three-way conflict (world fact in parameters, role belief in context or persona, decision), not studied.

## Decodability ≠ use (2025–2026 methodological papers)
- **Decodability is Not Causality** (2609.18080, COLM-26 workshop): SAE decomposition of a truth probe; probe-heavy features and behaviour-sensitive features overlap weakly (top-16 share 2); shared features flip outputs far more than probe-only ones.
- **Decodable but Not Corrected** (2605.05715): overthinking in medical QA is decodable at 71.6% but 29 fixed linear steering configs give Δ≈0; 88% of the contrastive vector is a generic "incorrect-vs-correct" direction; a learned non-linear intervention partially helps. Cites a "representation–behavior gap" in tool-calling agents (99% probe AUC, model still fails to act).
- **Do LLMs know internally when they follow instructions?** (Heo et al., 2410.14516): an input-embedding dimension predicts instruction-following success; generalises across tasks but not across instruction types; linked to prompt phrasing.
- **LLMs Know More Than They Show** (Orgad et al., 2410.02707): truthfulness signals concentrated at exact answer tokens; probes generalise poorly across tasks; models can encode the right answer yet output a wrong one.
- **For us:** (1) a probe success on "character knows F" is the *start*, never a finding; (2) the "decodable but steering does nothing" outcome is common and publishable only with a careful explanation; (3) probe generalisation across task families is usually poor — train/test split by scenario family is mandatory (already in the direction document).

## Knowing vs applying in ToM (behavioural)
- SimpleToM, T4D, Belief–behaviour Trust Game: see 02_datasets.md. Consensus: explicit mental-state inference is much easier than using it for behaviour; question-specific reminders close the gap → suggests the needed inference is often *not computed or not routed* in the applied task, rather than unknown. No mechanistic study of *why* exists yet (as of this reading).

## Lampinen, Li, Hosseini, Bhardwaj, Shanahan — Linear representations in language models can change dramatically over a conversation (GDM, 2601.20834)
- Gemma-3 (4B/12B/27B) and others; factuality (and ethics) directions found robustly (even disentangled from an "opposite day" prompt). Over a simulated conversation, **context-relevant** statements can flip factual ↔ non-factual along the direction, generic statements are preserved. In a two-role debate (model consciousness) the factuality readout flips with each role. Replaying an off-policy script produces similar changes. Bigger models change more (little in 4B). Steering along a direction can have *opposite* behavioural effects at different points of the conversation.
- **Tension with Sturgeon et al.:** Sturgeon finds single-turn persona prompting barely moves the truth direction (but a small, selective "protection gap" for persona-endorsed falsehoods); Lampinen finds multi-turn role context moves context-relevant factuality strongly. Both agree the change is content-selective. Likely reconcilers: number of turns / whether the role is *enacted* in the context, statement placement (user turn vs within conversation), model.
- **For us:** a probe for "character believes F" trained under one prompt mode (Knowing) may not mean the same thing under another mode (Enacting) or later in a dialogue. Cross-mode probe transfer is itself a measurement, not an assumption; causal tests (patching) are needed before interpreting any probe readout.
